# Shared 17-test rubric

Score each test 0–4. Half points are not allowed. Write the number and one observed fact.

| Score | Meaning |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Absent or inverted. The opposite of the brief. |
| 1 | Present as a leftover. Fails at a glance. |
| 2 | Partial. Works in one size or one still only. |
| 3 | Holds. A stranger can name the read. |
| 4 | Owned. Specific to this character and this page. |

Specialist skills reuse these rows. Do not keep a second scoring table.

## Tests

1. **Silhouette.** Identifiable as a black blob. Favicon, far-scroll hero, sticker thumbnail. Hats, hair masses, and ears count as silhouette tools.
2. **Shape family.** One dominant primitive (circle for warm or cute) plus one contrast family. Interior folds echo the silhouette.
3. **Kindchenschema slider.** Large head, high forehead, large low-set eyes, small nose and mouth, short thick limbs, soft rounded forms. Score the *slider*, not max kawaii. Too much schema is a generic baby. Too little on a toy brief is a mannequin.
4. **Appeal is not cute.** Interesting to watch, or just baby-schema? Mirrored left and right (twins) look dead. Pretty is not enough.
5. **Color blocking.** Two to four masses readable at favicon, sticker, and hero sizes. Fashion blocking yes. Nine-head croquis no.
6. **Squint / value.** One clear focal point, usually the face. Desaturate the still. If everything is the same gray, the light failed.
7. **Sticker reduction.** Identity survives transparent PNG, rotation, tiny size, and a busy ground. Objects-only and pale low-contrast stamps fail.
8. **Face kit.** Lids and brows carry thought. Eyes are not dead spheres. Upper lids are alertness. Lower lids are intensity. Orbit says feeling. Eyeballs say where.
9. **Hair as mass.** Silhouette first. One or two large clumps plus one break plus one highlight. Not a helmet. Not 200k photoreal strands.
10. **CMF.** Color, material, finish. Reads as toy, sticker, clay, vinyl, or plush. Not scanned skin. Finish is matte toy versus greasy spec.
11. **Light.** Warm-to-cool, readable terminator, rim that lifts the character, simplified shadows. Shadows go cool, not black. Light from above. Identifiable with shading off.
12. **Motion.** Squash with conserved volume. Anticipation. Arcs. Overlap on hair or cloth. No twinning. One exaggeration hierarchy. One hero beat.
13. **Site chrome.** UI supports the character. Nav, type, and cards stay quiet. Chrome must not photoreal-glass the cartoon. One metaphor. No decorative collage that kills the hero.
14. **Design-specificity.** Could another product wear this unchanged? Did we spend the free axis on an anti-default?
15. **2-second first impression.** Character versus chrome versus sticker noise. Purpose clear.
16. **Norman three levels.** Visceral aww, behavioral site-and-motion, reflective “this is *their* character.” Cute that breaks scroll or contrast fails behavioral.
17. **Proportion pole.** Cute characters live at about 2 to 4 heads. IBM 8-head plus “no cartoon” is the anti-pattern. Elongated fashion figures are the other anti-pattern.

## How to use a score

A 0 or 1 on tests 1, 14, or 15 is a P0. Fix identity before polish.

When the artifact is a *site with no character yet*, score the missing character rows as 0 and say so. Do not invent a face to score.

n/a is allowed only for test 8–9 when there is no figure, and you still mark 0 if the brief required a figure.
